How to type π, ≠, ∞, ± and other math symbols on a Mac

Your Mac keyboard already has nearly twenty math symbols on its Option layer, from π to ∫. Here’s every one, the Greek look-alikes that fool search, and what to do about ×, ², ∈ and the other symbols Apple left off.

By Wells Riley, who makes Mojito·Updated October 4, 2026·Tested on macOS 27

⌥option P → π

Same key on the US and British layouts.

How do I type the pi symbol on a Mac?

Press Option + P and macOS types π, in any app, with no setup. The same Option layer holds most of the math you’d put in a sentence: Option + = is ≠, Option + 5 is ∞, Option + X is ≈, and Shift + Option + = is ±.

Most of them sit roughly where you’d guess. ≠ and ± share the equals key, ≤ and ≥ share keys with < and >, ÷ is on the slash, π is on P, and √ is on V, which it looks like.

With Mojito, you type ::pi, ::not_equal, or ::plus_minus, and the symbols with no key work the same way: ::times for ×, ::squared for ², ::element_of for ∈.

Is π the only Greek letter on the keyboard?

No, whatever you may have read. Option + Z types a real capital omega, Ω. Four more keys type characters that look Greek but are, as far as Unicode is concerned, separate math symbols:

TypesKeyUnicode nameGreek letterMojito
∆⌥ JIncrementΔ::Delta
∑⌥ WN-ary summationΣ::Sigma
∏⇧ ⌥ PN-ary productΠ::Pi
µ⌥ MMicro signμ::mu

To a reader they’re identical. To a computer they’re different characters, so searching for one usually won’t find the other, and a font is free to draw them differently. For a quick ∆x in an email, ⌥ J is fine. For Greek text, or anything that will be searched or typeset, use the real letter.

The rest of the alphabet (α, β, θ, λ, σ, ω and friends) has no key on the US layout. You have three routes:

  • Character Viewer: Greek isn’t in the sidebar by default. In the expanded viewer, open the Action menu, choose Customize List, and turn on Greek.
  • The Greek layout: add it under System SettingsKeyboardText InputEdit… and switch to it from the Input menu. Most letters map by sound (L types λ, D types δ), with a few surprises, like θ on U.
  • Mojito: ::alpha through ::omega, and ::lambda works even though Unicode spells it “lamda.” Capitals have their own codes (::Delta, ::Sigma, ::Omega); type the name and both cases show up in the picker, lowercase first.

4 ways to type the symbols with no key

  1. The Character Viewer: press Fn + E (or Control + Command + Space), click the expand button in the panel’s top-right corner, and open Math Symbols in the sidebar. ×, ∈, ∪, ∀ and the rest are there; double-click one to insert it. Arrows have their own category, and superscripts are quickest found by searching for “superscript.”
  2. Unicode Hex Input: add it in System SettingsKeyboardText InputEdit… (click + and search for it), switch to it, then hold Option and type the code. Option + 00D7 types ×, 00B2 types ².
  3. A text replacement: in System SettingsKeyboardText Replacements…, map something like ;x to ×. It syncs to your iPhone, but it only fires in apps built on Apple’s text system, so not in Chrome, Slack, or VS Code.
  4. A shortcode: with Mojito installed, type ::times or ::squ in any text field and press Return. It also converts typed arrows as you go: -> becomes →, <- becomes ←, and <-> becomes ↔.

Should x² be formatting or a character?

In a document, formatting is usually the better choice. In TextEdit, select the 2 and choose FormatFontBaselineSuperscript. Pages has shortcuts: ⌃ ⇧ ⌘ + for superscript and ⌃ ⌘ - for subscript.

Formatting disappears the moment text goes somewhere plain, though: a message, a spreadsheet cell, a filename, a code comment. The characters ² and ₂ survive anywhere. Unicode only has superscript and subscript digits plus a patchy set of letters, so anything past x² or H₂O wants formatting or an equation.

For real equations, use an equation editor. In Pages, choose InsertEquation and write it in LaTeX or MathML; double-click it later to edit. Word has InsertEquation, and Google Docs has InsertSymbolsEquation.

More math symbol questions

How do I type the multiplication sign (×) on a Mac?

There’s no key for it. Open the Character Viewer’s Math Symbols list, use Unicode Hex Input (Option + 00D7), or type ::times with Mojito. A lowercase x reads fine in casual writing; in anything typeset, the real × is easier on the eye.

Why does Option-V type √ instead of a check mark?

Option + V is the square root sign, and macOS has no check mark key at all. Typing a real ✓ takes the Character Viewer or a shortcode.

My Option shortcuts don’t work in Terminal. Why?

Terminal’s Use Option as Meta key setting, under TerminalSettingsProfilesKeyboard, sends Option to the shell instead of typing symbols. Turn it off and ⌥P is π again.

What are the HTML codes for math symbols?

&pi; π, &ne; ≠, &le; ≤, &ge; ≥, &plusmn; ±, &times; ×, &divide; ÷, &minus; −, &infin; ∞, &radic; √, and &sum; ∑.

Why does :infinity: give me ♾️ instead of ∞?

In Mojito, one colon searches emoji and two search symbols. :infinity: is the emoji; ::infinity is the math symbol. Hyphens and degree signs have look-alikes too: see the guides to the minus sign and dashes and the degree symbol.

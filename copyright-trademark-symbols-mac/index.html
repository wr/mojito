How to type ©, ™ and ® on a Mac

Option-G for ©, Option-2 for ™, Option-R for ®, in any app. Plus ℠, ℗, and which mark you’re allowed to use.

By Wells Riley of Wells Workshop, maker of Mojito·Updated October 5, 2026·Tested on macOS 27

⌥option G → ©

⌥ 2 types ™ · ⌥ R types ®

How do I type the copyright symbol on a Mac?

Press Option + G for ©, Option + 2 for ™, and Option + R for ®. They work in every app, with no setup.

Those are the US keys. On a British keyboard, Option + 2 types the euro sign, and ™ moves to Shift + Option + 2.

Mojito lets you type ::copy, ::tm, or ::reg instead, plus ::sm for ℠, which no keyboard has.

Other ways to type them

  • Character Viewer. Press Fn + E to open the emoji picker, click the expand button (top right), search copyright, trademark, or registered, and double-click the result. That’s where ℠ and ℗ live too.
  • Type (c). Pages, Numbers, Keynote, and Word turn (c) into © as you type. Mail, Notes, TextEdit, and Safari leave it alone.
  • Text replacement. In System SettingsKeyboardText Replacements…, map ;tm to ™. Syncs to your iPhone, but only works in Apple’s apps.
  • Shortcode. With Mojito, type ::copy, ::tm, or ::reg in any app. ::sm gives you ℠, and ::sound finds ℗.

™ or ®? What each mark means

  • ™ Trademark: claims a name, logo, or slogan for your goods. You can use it without registering.
  • ℠ Service mark: the same claim, for services.
  • ® Registered: only once the mark is registered (in the US, by the USPTO), not while the application is pending.
  • © Copyright: optional in the US since 1989, and no registration needed. Format: © 2026 Northwind Studio.
  • ℗ Sound recording copyright: the © for the audio itself.

That’s US guidance from the Copyright Office and USPTO, not legal advice.

How do I type ©, ™ and ® on iPhone?

There’s no key for them. Set up a text replacement (ones from your Mac sync over), or use the ©️ ™️ ®️ emoji.

More © ™ ® questions

How do I stop (c) turning into ©?

In Pages, Numbers, or Keynote, turn off Symbol and text substitution under PagesSettingsAuto-Correction, or delete just the (c) row. In Word, delete the (c) entry from WordPreferencesAutoCorrect.

Should ® be superscript?

Often: © and ® sit at full size, which can look heavy next to a brand name. In Pages, select the mark and press ⌃ ⇧ ⌘ +; on the web, wrap it in <sup>.

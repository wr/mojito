How to send GIFs on a Mac

Messages has GIF search built in. Most other Mac apps don’t, which is where frozen GIFs and mystery .webp files come from. Here’s how to get one that actually moves into whatever you’re writing.

By Wells Riley, who makes Mojito·Updated October 4, 2026·Tested on macOS 27

+apps #images → GIF

In Messages: the Apps button left of the message field, then #images. Search, click, press Return.

How do I send a GIF in Messages on a Mac?

Open a conversation in Messages, click the Apps button (+) to the left of the message field, and choose #images. Pick a trending GIF or search by keyword, click one to add it to your message, and press Return to send. #images is available in 13 countries, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and India.

With Mojito, GIF search works outside Messages too: type :::party in Mail or a browser, and Return pastes the GIF right where you’re typing.

Good to know about #images

  • It animates for Apple users. Apple’s own note: GIFs from #images animate only in Messages on Apple devices. A friend reading on Android shouldn’t count on it moving.
  • Saving one you received: Control-click the GIF and choose Save. It goes to the Photos app, not your Downloads folder. For a file you can attach somewhere else, drag it from the conversation to your desktop.
  • No #images? Apple lists 13 countries: the US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, France, Germany, Italy, India, Singapore, the Philippines, and Japan. Its advice (written for iPhone) is to check that your language and region are one of those; on a Mac that’s System SettingsGeneralLanguage & Region.
  • Any GIF file works too. Drag a .gif from Finder into the message field, or copy and paste it.

Is there a GIF keyboard for Mac?

Not built in, and strictly speaking not on iPhone either. #images is an app inside Messages, not part of the keyboard; GIF search in the iPhone keyboard itself means a third-party keyboard such as Google’s Gboard. The Mac doesn’t have add-on keyboards like that, so GIF search lives inside individual apps: Messages, Slack, Discord, most social sites. Everywhere else you fetch GIFs by hand (below) or use a tool that puts search at your cursor, like Mojito; other apps that do GIFs are compared in the best emoji apps for Mac. Emoji, at least, do have a system-wide shortcut: Fn-E.

What happened to Tenor GIFs?

On June 30, 2026, Google shut down the public Tenor API, the service that let other companies’ apps search Tenor’s library. GIF buttons that depended on it broke or switched providers overnight; Discord’s now runs on KLIPY. Tenor itself is still here: tenor.com searches fine in any Mac browser, and Google’s own Gboard still uses it. On a Mac, Tenor GIFs now come from the website, using the save-and-drag routine below. The full timeline, and where each app’s GIFs come from now, is in Tenor GIF keyboard for Mac: what happened.

How do I send GIFs in Slack, Discord, Mail, and other apps?

  • Slack: click the smiley face in the message field, choose GIFs, search, pick one, and send. It’s powered by Giphy, and workspace admins can turn it off, so if GIFs aren’t in that menu, that’s why. The older /giphy command only works if your workspace has the Giphy app installed.
  • Discord: click the GIF button in the message bar. Since the Tenor shutdown, those results come from KLIPY.
  • Mail: drag a .gif file into the body of your message. Most mail apps play it, but classic desktop Outlook for Windows shows only the first frame, so make sure that frame works on its own.
  • Websites: use the site’s own GIF button if it has one. If not, attach the .gif with its upload button, or drag the file into the compose box.
  • Notes, Pages, Keynote: drag the file in like any other image.

Getting a real .gif from Giphy or Tenor

Find a GIF on giphy.com, right-click, choose Save Image As, and check the file name: it often ends in .webp. Both Giphy and Tenor send browsers WebP or silent MP4 video instead of GIF, because it’s much smaller: a Giphy WebP can be half the size of the same .gif. Your browser plays it fine. Plenty of apps and upload forms that take GIFs won’t accept it, or won’t animate it.

On Giphy, the fix takes ten seconds: right-click the GIF, choose Open Image in New Tab, change .webp at the end of the address to .gif, press Return, and save that. On Tenor, open the GIF’s own page first; the big image there is a real .gif, while thumbnails in search results often aren’t. Then drag the file from Downloads into whatever you’re sending. Dragging hands over the file itself, which is what keeps it moving.

Why do GIFs arrive frozen?

Because what arrived wasn’t really a GIF file. The four usual reasons:

  • It was pasted as a picture. Copy Image hands the clipboard image data, and many apps keep only the first frame. Chrome-based desktop apps, Slack and Discord among them, flatten pasted image data to a still. Paste or drag the file instead.
  • It was a link. Copying a giphy.com page address sends a URL. Some apps unfurl it into a moving preview; plenty show a card or a bare link.
  • It wasn’t a GIF. A .webp or .mp4 can play in a browser and nowhere else. Check the extension before you send.
  • Motion is off on the other end. With Auto-play animated images turned off in System SettingsAccessibilityMotion, a Mac pauses GIFs in Messages and Safari. If GIFs never move for you, check that switch first.

Search GIFs at your cursor with Mojito

Messages has #images, and Slack and Discord have their own buttons. Mail, Notes, Pages, and most websites have nothing. Mojito fills that gap with a third colon: type :::party and a panel of GIFs from KLIPY opens at your cursor. Arrow keys move through them, and Return pastes the one you picked. It pastes the GIF itself, a real .gif file rather than a still or a link, so it animates anywhere GIFs are allowed to.

GIF search is the only time anything you type in Mojito leaves your Mac. The words after ::: go to KLIPY to fetch results, along with your country so they suit where you are, and nothing that ties searches to you. Emoji and symbol shortcodes like :tada: and ::degree (the full list) never touch the network. Rather not? Turn off GIF search in Mojito’s SettingsGeneral.

More GIF questions

Does the iPhone keyboard have GIF search?

Apple’s doesn’t. On iPhone, GIFs come from #images inside Messages: tap the + button, then #images. To search GIFs from the keyboard itself in any app, you need a third-party keyboard such as Gboard.

Why don’t GIFs move in Messages on my Mac?

Most likely Auto-play animated images is off in System SettingsAccessibilityMotion. If only one GIF is still, the sender probably pasted a still image rather than the file.

Do I need Tenor or Giphy accounts to send GIFs?

No. Searching giphy.com and tenor.com and saving GIFs works without signing in, and #images in Messages needs nothing beyond a supported region.

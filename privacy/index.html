Mojito and your privacy

Mojito reads your keystrokes. Here’s every permission it asks for, everything it sends, and everything it keeps, checked against its code.

By Wells Riley of Wells Workshop, maker of Mojito·Updated October 5, 2026·Checked against Mojito 1.10.0

Is Mojito safe to give keystroke access?

Mojito watches for shortcodes like :tada: on your Mac. What you type isn’t saved or sent anywhere, except GIF search words, which go to KLIPY. It never opens in password fields, and it’s open source.

Why does Mojito need Accessibility and Input Monitoring?

Input Monitoring: seeing the shortcode

  • Watches for :, ::, :::, or :). Ignores ⌘, ⌃, and ⌥ combinations, except ⌘ Z.
  • Holds only the shortcode you’re typing (until you insert or cancel) and the current word (until a space or a second’s pause), in memory.
  • Can swallow keys, so Return and the arrows drive the open picker. Never in excluded apps; the hook is removed while paused.

Accessibility: finding your cursor and inserting the emoji

  • Find your cursor, reading only the one character before it, not your document.
  • Check the field type, to stay out of password fields.
  • Read the website’s address, for per-site exclusions. Only the host name is matched.
  • Insert the emoji by typing it like a keyboard, or by pasting a GIF.

Everything else

  • Automation, Arc only: Arc hides its address bar from Accessibility, so Mojito asks Arc for the tab’s address with AppleScript. macOS asks you first.
  • Never requested: Screen Recording, Full Disk Access, camera, microphone, location, or contacts.

Password fields and Secure Input

  • Password fields, and any field Mojito can’t identify: no picker, GIF search, or pasting.
  • Secure Input: when an app turns it on (Terminal’s Secure Keyboard Entry, some password managers and VPN clients), macOS blocks every keystroke tool, Mojito included. Find the app responsible.

What Mojito keeps on your Mac

  • Settings: triggers, features, excluded apps and sites, and whether it’s paused.
  • Emoji counts: one undated count per emoji, for ranking. Plus lifetime totals and the date you first opened it.
  • Favorites and custom aliases.
  • GIFs: up to 50 MB of cached results. Each GIF you paste sits in a temporary folder for a few minutes, and its filename includes your search word, because chat apps show it on upload.

None of it is the text you type. The closest is which emoji you use, your alias words, and, briefly, a GIF search word.

On by default, but nothing is sent until Mojito has shown a one-time notice; choose “Not now” there, or switch stats off any time. A report contains only this:

  • Never sent: anything you type, apps or sites, GIF searches, aliases, timestamps, or any ID linking one day’s report to the next.
  • On the server: it never reads your IP address or device details, and stores only daily totals, all public at /stats.
  • Next release: three yes/no answers worked out on your Mac (new install? used this week? this month?), to count active users with no ID.

GIF search and KLIPY

Each ::: search sends KLIPY:

  • Your search words, after a short typing pause, so a half-typed word can go too.
  • Your country, to localize results.
  • An app key that’s the same for everyone.
  • The results page, 24 results, KLIPY’s “medium” rating, and “GIFs only.”

Mojito keeps no list of your searches. GIF search works even in apps Mojito otherwise skips, like Slack and Discord, unless you turn that off.

How to check all this yourself

  • Read the code. The keyboard hook, field checks and network code are all on GitHub.
  • Watch its connections with LuLu, Little Snitch, or lsof -nP -i -a -c Mojito: mojito.wells.ee and github.com (updates), api.klipy.com and KLIPY’s image servers (GIF search only), stats.mojito.wells.ee (at most daily).
  • Check the signature. Notarized by Apple, with signed updates and a checksum-pinned Homebrew cask.

Privacy questions

Does Mojito sell or share data?

No. There’s no account or ads, and the only usage data is the public counts above. Besides hosts GitHub and Cloudflare, KLIPY is the only outside company involved, and it only sees GIF searches.

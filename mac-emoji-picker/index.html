The Mac emoji picker, and the alternatives

Every Mac has an emoji picker one shortcut away, and it does more than most people ever use. Here’s all of it, where it falls short, and what to try if you want something faster.

By Wells Riley, who makes Mojito·Updated October 4, 2026·Tested on macOS 27

How do I open the emoji picker on a Mac?

Click in any text field and press Fn + E, or Control + Command + Space. The emoji picker opens next to your cursor. Type to search, then click an emoji or pick one with the arrow keys and Return. No shortcut in your head? Choose EditEmoji & Symbols from the menu bar instead; nearly every app has it.

With Mojito, you can skip the panel: type :coffee where you’re writing and press Return. It can also put its own keyboard-first emoji browser on ⌃ ⌘ Space.

What the built-in picker can do

It’s better than its reputation. Most people use the search box and nothing else, which leaves a lot on the table:

  • Search by name or idea. pizza, party, and thumbs all work, and the results update as you type.
  • Frequently Used. The emoji you pick most float to the top, so your regulars stay close.
  • Skin tones. Click and hold an emoji that has variants, then choose one. macOS remembers the tone for that emoji, not for all of them.
  • Word to emoji. Since macOS 14, you can type heart first and then press Fn + E; macOS suggests matches, and your pick replaces the word.
  • Stickers and Genmoji. Your stickers live in the picker. On Macs with Apple Intelligence, the Genmoji button at the top right makes a custom emoji from a description, and it’s saved alongside your stickers.
  • The full Character Viewer. The expand button in the top-right corner turns the compact picker into a larger window with categories and thousands of symbols beyond emoji. Double-click to insert there; single click in the compact picker.
  • Favorites. In the expanded viewer, select an emoji and click Add to Favorites. Favorites sit at the top of the Character Viewer.
  • Housekeeping. The expanded viewer’s Action menu can customize the category list (turn on Technical Symbols to get ⌘ ⌥ ⇧) and clear Frequently Used.

What changed in macOS 26 and 27?

Mostly the look. macOS 26 restyled the picker with Apple’s glass material and macOS 27 keeps it; the shortcuts and settings are the same ones that worked on macOS 14. Genmoji got an overhaul in macOS 27, with its button at the top right of the Character Viewer.

The emoji themselves did change: macOS 26.4 added Emoji 17.0, including Orca 🫍, Trombone 🪊, and Treasure Chest 🪎, for 3,953 in total. Send one to an older Mac or phone and it may show up as an empty box.

Where the built-in picker falls short

  • It’s a detour. Every emoji is a round trip: open the panel, search, pick, find your place again. A few times a day, nobody minds. In a busy chat, it adds up.
  • No shortcodes. Slack and GitHub taught your fingers :tada:. In Mail, Notes, or Messages, that stays plain text.
  • You don’t run Frequently Used. macOS decides what goes there. You can clear it, not arrange it, and favorites are added one at a time in the expanded viewer.
  • One skin tone at a time. There’s no default tone for every emoji; you set it per emoji as you go.
  • No GIFs. The picker stops at emoji, symbols, and stickers. For GIFs, see how to send GIFs on a Mac.
  • Secure Input turns it off. When an app switches on Secure Keyboard Entry (Terminal’s option, some VPN clients, background password managers), the picker won’t open anywhere, Edit menu included. Fair warning: that blocks every third-party picker too.

Alternatives to the Mac emoji picker

Third-party pickers come in two shapes. Which one suits you depends on where you want the emoji to appear: at your cursor, or in a launcher window.

Inline autocomplete: a colon, a few letters, Return

These watch for a colon and show a small list at your cursor, the way Slack does. Your eyes never leave the line you’re writing.

  • Mojito (ours): free and open source (AGPL), macOS 14 or later. : for emoji, with search by meaning as well as by name; :: for symbols; ::: for GIF search through KLIPY; :? for favorites you pin.
  • Rocket: free, and still updated (it added Emoji 17 in March 2026). A one-time $10 Rocket Pro adds a search-and-browse window, GIFs and images you save, custom shortcuts, and snippets. Closed source, macOS 13.5 or later.
  • Comoji: free and closed source, macOS 13 or later. An inline : picker, plus /gif (GIPHY) and /sticker at the cursor, and a :: emoji browser.

Launcher pickers: Raycast and Alfred

Raycast’s emoji and symbol search is free, but results appear in Raycast’s own launcher window rather than at your cursor; its : trigger only works inside Raycast. Alfred can expand exact :codes: through community snippet packs, which needs its paid Powerpack (£34) and gives you no autocomplete list. If a launcher is already part of your day, either is a sensible pick.

Can I replace the Mac emoji picker?

Yes, and keep the shortcut. Mojito can take over ⌃ ⌘ Space and a tap of the 🌐 key so they open its emoji browser instead of Apple’s panel: every emoji in a grid, with the cursor already in the search field. Type a few letters, arrow to the one you want, press Return. ⇧ Return inserts and keeps the browser open for the next one, and Esc closes it.

  1. Install Mojito and open its settings.
  2. In SettingsGeneral, click Replace System Picker.
  3. If it asks, log out and back in once to finish handing over the 🌐 key.

Changed your mind? The reset button next to the shortcut gives Apple’s picker its keys back.

Emoji picker questions

Where is the emoji picker on a Mac?

It isn’t an app in your Applications folder. It’s a system panel that opens in whatever app you’re typing in, from EditEmoji & Symbols or the shortcuts above. If you turn on Show Input menu in menu bar (in System SettingsKeyboardText InputEdit…), the Input menu can open it too.

How do I clear frequently used emoji?

Expand the picker to the full Character Viewer, open its Action menu, and choose Clear Frequently Used Characters.

Is there a Mac emoji picker that works like Slack’s?

Yes. Inline autocomplete apps (Mojito, Rocket, Comoji) bring the :shortcode: habit to every app. The emoji shortcode cheat sheet lists the codes worth knowing.

Will a third-party picker see my passwords?

Mojito skips password fields on purpose, and its matching happens on your Mac. When Secure Keyboard Entry is on, no picker works at all, Apple’s included.

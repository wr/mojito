The best emoji app for Mac: six options compared

Six ways to get emoji into whatever you’re typing, from the picker Apple ships to apps that autocomplete :shortcodes: the way Slack does. There’s no single winner, so this ends with a pick for each kind of typist.

By Wells Riley, who makes Mojito·Updated October 4, 2026·Tested on macOS 27

Disclosure: I make Mojito, one of the six. So the built-in picker goes first, every price and feature below comes from the vendor’s own site or changelog, and the verdict says plainly where something else is the better pick. Prices and features verified October 4, 2026.

What’s the best emoji app for Mac?

For most people, the one already installed: press Fn + E (or Control + Command + Space) and pick from the panel. If you type emoji every day, an inline picker that turns :fire into 🔥 as you type is faster. Rocket is the polished veteran, Comoji adds free GIFs and stickers, and Mojito is the free, open-source one that also types symbols.

Already use Raycast? Its emoji search is free and probably all you need.

What is each one good at?

The built-in picker (free, already on your Mac)

Press Fn + E and a panel opens at your cursor. Type to search (party, thumbs, pizza), then press Return. Since macOS Sonoma you can also type the word first, press Fn + E, and swap it for a matching emoji. The expand button opens the full Character Viewer, which has symbols as well as emoji. It’s the only option here with Genmoji.

The cost is the interruption. Every emoji is a small detour through a panel, there are no :shortcodes: to build muscle memory around, and GIF search exists only in Messages. For a few emoji a day, none of that matters. All seven built-in routes are in How to type emoji on a Mac.

Rocket (free, or $10 once for Pro)

Matthew Palmer’s Rocket has made Slack-style shortcodes work across the Mac since 2016: type :thumbsup in any app and a list appears at your cursor. The free version does that well. A one-time $10 Pro upgrade adds a search-and-browse window, about 150 saved GIFs, images and memes you can add to, custom shortcuts, text snippets and usage stats.

Two corrections to older comparisons, including the one that used to be on this page: Rocket has no symbol catalog (Pro lets you attach your own symbols to shortcuts), and its GIFs are a saved collection, not an online search. It’s closed source, still maintained (1.9.5 added Emoji 17 in March 2026), and runs on macOS 13.5 or later.

Raycast (emoji free; Pro $10 a month)

Raycast is a Spotlight replacement, and its Search Emoji & Symbols command is part of the free core. Open it with a hotkey, search by name or description, and Return pastes the result into the app you were using. It covers Unicode symbols as well as emoji.

Results appear in Raycast’s window, not at your cursor, and its : trigger only works inside Raycast’s own text fields. Pro ($10 a month or $96 a year, with Plus at $20 and Max at $50 a month) pays for AI, cloud sync and unlimited clipboard history, not emoji; the free plan keeps three months of clipboard history. GIF search comes from community extensions. One catch for older Macs: Raycast 2, which replaced the original in August 2026, needs macOS Tahoe on Apple silicon.

Comoji (free)

Comoji, by Matt Senter, works like Rocket: type : and a few letters, pick from a list at the cursor, press Tab or Return. Typing :: opens a bigger emoji browser. /gif searches GIPHY and /sticker inserts stickers, both at the cursor in Messages, Mail, WhatsApp and browsers. It’s free and closed source, needs macOS 13, and leaves terminals, password managers and virtual machines alone by default. There’s no symbol search. The full side-by-side is in Mojito vs Comoji.

Alfred (needs the £34 Powerpack)

Alfred is a launcher like Raycast, with no emoji picker of its own. The usual route is a snippet pack, such as Joel Califa’s, that expands exact codes: type :tada: and it becomes 🎉. There’s no list to choose from, so you have to know the code, and snippets need the paid Powerpack (£34, or £59 as a Mega Supporter). If you already own it, this costs nothing extra. If you don’t, it’s an expensive way to type emoji.

Mojito (free, open source)

Mojito is mine, so weigh what follows accordingly. It works at the cursor like Rocket and Comoji, with three triggers: : for emoji, :: for symbols, ::: for GIFs. Emoji search matches meaning as well as names, with about 8,300 everyday keywords, so :deploy finds 🚀 and :ghosting finds 👻. ::deg types ° and ::cmd types ⌘; none of the others search symbols at the cursor. GIFs come from KLIPY and paste as a real GIF file.

It’s free with no paid tier, AGPL open source, and runs on macOS 14 or later on Apple silicon and Intel. It stays out of Slack, Discord and other apps with their own emoji picker, so their native codes keep working, and out of terminals and code editors unless you switch them on. The trade-offs: no stickers, no Genmoji, no text snippets, and it’s young (1.0 shipped in May 2026).

Raycast or Rocket for emoji?

They’re built for different moments. Rocket watches what you type, so :fire becomes 🔥 without your hands leaving the sentence. Raycast is a detour: hotkey, search, Return, and it pastes back into your app. In exchange it searches symbols too, and if you already open Raycast fifty times a day, the detour is muscle memory. Rocket also runs on Intel Macs and macOS 13.5, where Raycast 2 doesn’t. Nothing stops you using both.

Which one should you pick?

  • You add an emoji now and then: stay with the built-in picker. Fn + E, a word, Return. It’s also the only way to make a Genmoji.
  • You already live in Raycast: use its Search Emoji & Symbols command and give it a hotkey. A second app just for emoji isn’t worth it.
  • You want the proven inline picker: Rocket. The free version covers the basics; pay the $10 if you’ll use snippets or a saved GIF library.
  • You send a lot of GIFs and stickers in Messages, Mail or WhatsApp: Comoji does both for free, and no other inline picker has an equivalent of its /sticker.
  • You want free and open source, or you type symbols as often as emoji: Mojito. Writers reaching for — and °, and anyone documenting ⌘ shortcuts, get the most out of ::.

And if you already own Alfred’s Powerpack and know your shortcodes by heart, a snippet pack is a free add-on.

Whichever you try, run one inline picker at a time. Two apps both listening for a colon will trip over each other, and :: means different things in Comoji (emoji browser) and Mojito (symbols).

Common questions

Is there a free emoji picker for Mac?

Yes, several. The built-in one costs nothing and needs no setup: Fn + E. For inline :shortcode: autocomplete, Rocket’s free version, Comoji and Mojito are all free, and Raycast’s emoji command is free too. Of those, Mojito is the only one that’s open source.

Why do emoji apps need Accessibility and Input Monitoring?

An app that autocompletes as you type has to see your keystrokes to notice :fire (that’s Input Monitoring), then find the text field and put the emoji at your cursor (that’s Accessibility). No inline picker can work without the pair. It’s also why every inline picker goes quiet when Secure Keyboard Entry is on, which Terminal, some VPN clients and password managers can switch on. Are third-party emoji apps safe?

Keystroke access is real trust, so check three things: that the app skips password fields, what it sends over the network, and how you’d know. Open source helps with the last one. Anyone can read Mojito’s code: its only network traffic is the update check, GIF searches you run, and anonymous usage stats (you’re asked once, and can turn them off) that are public. With closed-source apps you take the privacy page on trust, though Comoji publishes a guide to checking its traffic yourself.

Can I keep using ⌃⌘Space too?

Yes. Inline pickers listen for a colon, so Fn + E and Control + Command + Space keep opening Apple’s panel, which you’ll still want for Genmoji. Mojito can optionally take over ⌃⌘Space and the 🌐 key to open its own emoji browser; leave that off and nothing changes. More on that trade in the Mac emoji picker guide.

Is there a free, open-source alternative to Rocket?

Mojito: it autocompletes the same Slack-style codes at your cursor, adds symbol and GIF search, and is AGPL licensed. Comoji is the other free inline picker, but it’s closed source. Rocket still has things neither offers, like text snippets and a saved GIF library, and it runs on macOS 13.5, where Mojito needs 14.

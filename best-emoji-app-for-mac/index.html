The best emoji app for Mac: six options compared

Apple’s picker and five apps that autocomplete :shortcodes: the way Slack does, compared, with a pick for each kind of typist.

By Wells Riley, who makes Mojito·Updated October 5, 2026·Tested on macOS 27

Disclosure: I make Mojito, one of the six. The built-in picker goes first, every price and feature comes from the vendor’s own site or changelog, and the verdict says where something else is the better pick. Prices and features verified October 4, 2026.

What’s the best emoji app for Mac?

For most people, the one already installed: press Fn + E (or Control + Command + Space). If you type emoji every day, an inline picker that turns :fire into 🔥 as you type is faster.

Rocket is the polished veteran, Comoji adds free GIFs and stickers, and Mojito is the free, open-source one that also types symbols. Already use Raycast? Its free emoji search is probably all you need.

What is each one good at?

The built-in picker (free, already on your Mac)

  • Fn + E opens a searchable panel at your cursor. Since Sonoma, you can type a word first and swap it for an emoji.
  • The only one with Genmoji. Its expand button opens the Character Viewer, with symbols too.
  • The cost: a detour through a panel for every emoji, no :shortcodes:, and GIF search only in Messages. (All seven built-in routes.)

Rocket (free, or $10 once for Pro)

  • Inline shortcodes since 2016: type :thumbsup in any app and a list appears at your cursor.
  • Pro adds a search window, about 150 saved GIFs, images and memes, custom shortcuts, text snippets and usage stats.
  • No symbol catalog or GIF search: Pro lets you attach your own symbols, and its GIFs are a saved collection.
  • Closed source, still maintained (1.9.5 added Emoji 17 in March 2026), macOS 13.5 or later.

Raycast (emoji free; Pro $10 a month)

  • Search Emoji & Symbols is free: hotkey, search, and Return pastes into your app. It covers Unicode symbols too.
  • Results appear in Raycast’s window, not at your cursor. GIF search comes from community extensions.
  • Pro ($10 a month or $96 a year) pays for AI, cloud sync and unlimited clipboard history, not emoji.
  • Raycast 2 (August 2026) needs macOS Tahoe on Apple silicon.

Comoji (free)

  • Works like Rocket: type : and a few letters, then pick at the cursor. :: opens a bigger emoji browser.
  • /gif (GIPHY) and /sticker work at the cursor in Messages, Mail, WhatsApp and browsers.
  • Closed source, macOS 13, no symbol search. Terminals, password managers and virtual machines are off by default. (Mojito vs Comoji.)

Alfred (needs the £34 Powerpack)

  • No emoji picker: a snippet pack (such as Joel Califa’s) expands exact codes, so :tada: becomes 🎉. You have to know the code.
  • Snippets need the paid Powerpack (£34, or £59 as a Mega Supporter). No extra cost if you own it.

Mojito (free, open source)

  • Three triggers at the cursor: : for emoji, :: for symbols (::deg types °), ::: for GIFs from KLIPY.
  • Search by meaning: about 8,300 everyday keywords, so :deploy finds 🚀.
  • Free, no paid tier, AGPL, macOS 14 or later. Slack, Discord, terminals and code editors are off by default.
  • Trade-offs: no stickers, Genmoji or text snippets, and it’s young (1.0 shipped in May 2026).

Raycast or Rocket for emoji?

Rocket watches what you type, so :fire becomes 🔥 mid-sentence. Raycast is a detour (hotkey, search, Return), but it searches symbols too, and it’s muscle memory if you live in Raycast. Rocket also runs on Intel and macOS 13.5, where Raycast 2 doesn’t.

Which one should you pick?

  • You add an emoji now and then: the built-in picker. It’s also the only way to make a Genmoji.
  • You already live in Raycast: its Search Emoji & Symbols command, with a hotkey.
  • You want the proven inline picker: Rocket. Pay the $10 if you’ll use snippets or a saved GIF library.
  • You send lots of GIFs and stickers in Messages, Mail or WhatsApp: Comoji, free. No other inline picker has /sticker.
  • You want open source, or type symbols as often as emoji: Mojito. Writers reaching for — and °, and anyone documenting ⌘ shortcuts, get the most from ::.
  • You own Alfred’s Powerpack and know your shortcodes by heart: a snippet pack is a free add-on.

Run one inline picker at a time. Two apps listening for a colon trip over each other, and :: means different things in Comoji (emoji browser) and Mojito (symbols).

Common questions

Is there a free emoji picker for Mac?

Yes: Fn + E, and Raycast’s emoji command. For inline autocomplete, Rocket’s free version, Comoji and Mojito are free; only Mojito is open source.

Are third-party emoji apps safe?

Check that it skips password fields, what it sends, and how you’d know. Mojito’s code is public: it sends only update checks, your GIF searches, and anonymous stats (you’re asked once, and can turn them off) that are public. Comoji, closed source, publishes a guide to checking its traffic.

Is there a free, open-source alternative to Rocket?

Mojito: the same Slack-style codes at your cursor, plus symbols and GIFs, AGPL licensed. Rocket still has text snippets and a saved GIF library, and runs on macOS 13.5, where Mojito needs 14.

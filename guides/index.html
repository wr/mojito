Guides to typing emoji, symbols and GIFs on a Mac

Short, tested answers to the things your keyboard doesn’t show you: where the emoji shortcut went, how to type °, —, ✓ and ⌘, and how to get a GIF into something other than Messages.

By Wells Riley, who makes Mojito·Updated October 4, 2026·Tested on macOS 27

Each guide opens with the answer, usually one keystroke that’s been on your Mac all along, then covers what trips people up: the look-alike character that breaks a spreadsheet, the app that ignores the shortcut, the setting that changed by itself.

They’re for anyone who types on a Mac all day and keeps losing a minute to the Character Viewer: writers who want a real em dash, developers documenting ⌘ shortcuts, people who just want the 🎉 without the hunt. The built-in way always comes first, even when it’s the slow one.

Or skip the shortcuts

I make Mojito, which is why it turns up in most of these guides: it folds them all into one habit. One colon for emoji (:tada), two for symbols (::degree), three for GIF search (:::party), typed right where your cursor is. Every keystroke above still works without it.

One shortcode instead of fourteen guides

Mojito is a free, open-source Mac app for typing :tada:, ::degree, and :::party in almost any app.

Mojito vs Comoji

Two free Mac apps that autocomplete :shortcodes: at your cursor. Here’s where they differ, checked October 4, 2026.

By Wells Riley of Wells Workshop, maker of Mojito·Updated October 5, 2026·Comoji 1.1.12, Mojito 1.10.0

Disclosure: I make Mojito. Every Comoji fact here comes from comoji.io, which publishes its own Mojito comparison; where that’s out of date, this page says why.

Mojito or Comoji: which should you use?

Both are free and do the core job well. Choose Comoji for macOS 13 Ventura, animated stickers, or no usage data at all.

Choose Mojito for symbols like ⌘, °, — and €, emoji found by meaning, source code you can read, or a non-English interface.

What they have in common

Both are free with no account, use a colon trigger you can change, know Slack and Discord codes, keep an editable list of apps to stay out of, and need Input Monitoring and Accessibility. Run one at a time: with both, every : opens two pickers.

Where Comoji is the better pick

  • Older Macs. Comoji runs on macOS 13 Ventura; Mojito needs 14 Sonoma.
  • Animated stickers. /sticker inserts animated emoji stickers (Google’s Noto set) at your cursor.
  • No usage data, ever. Comoji says it has no analytics, telemetry, or crash reporter. Mojito’s anonymous stats are on by default after a one-time notice; you can decline there or switch them off later.
  • A checklist instead of source code. Comoji lists every server it talks to, plus a guide to checking that yourself: genuinely useful if you’d rather test than read code.
  • Stricter defaults. Comoji skips password managers, terminals, and remote-desktop or VM clients out of the box. Mojito skips password fields and terminals; you’d add password managers and VMs yourself.

Where Mojito is the better pick

  • Symbols. :: searches hundreds: ::cmd for ⌘, ::degree for °, plus currency, math, arrows, fractions, and Greek. Comoji’s :: opens an emoji browser.
  • Search by meaning. About 8,300 everyday words, so :deploy finds 🚀 and :ghosting finds 👻.
  • Source you can read, including the keystroke code. Comoji says it has declined to open its input path “for now.”
  • Small conversions. :) becomes 🙂 and -> becomes →, with no picker.
  • Favorites and the system shortcut. :? opens pinned favorites, and Mojito can take over ⌃ ⌘ Space and the 🌐 key.
  • Your language. The app is in 19 languages, and shortcodes work in 14, like :daumen_hoch for 👍.
  • Allowlist mode. Run Mojito only in the apps you name.

GIFs: GIPHY vs KLIPY

Both paste a real .gif at your cursor, so it animates. Comoji searches GIPHY; Mojito searches KLIPY, co-founded by one of Tenor’s co-founders (what happened to Tenor). Mojito’s also works in Slack and Discord, unless you switch that off.

Neither app sends your keystrokes anywhere. In both, GIF search words go to the GIF service. Beyond that:

About Comoji’s comparison page

Comoji’s Mojito comparison, checked September 11, 2026, is mostly fair. Four points have changed, or weren’t right:

  • “Mojito queries Giphy.” True when written. Mojito switched to KLIPY in 1.10.0, on October 1, 2026.
  • “The stand-down list is Mojito’s call rather than yours.” It’s yours: add, remove, or flip it into allowlist mode in Settings.
  • No custom trigger key. Every Mojito trigger (emoji, symbol, GIF, favorites) has been customizable since 1.5.
  • Permissions not published. A fair point about our homepage. They’re Input Monitoring and Accessibility, explained above and in the README.

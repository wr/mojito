How to type the degree symbol on a Mac (°)

Hold Shift and Option, press 8. That works in every app on your Mac. The rest of this page covers the two look-alikes a few keys over, whether °F should be one character or two, and what to press when your keyboard isn’t US English.

By Wells Riley, who makes Mojito·Updated October 4, 2026·Tested on macOS 27

⇧shift ⌥option 8 → °

How do I type the degree symbol on a Mac?

Press Shift + Option + 8. macOS types the degree sign, °, wherever your cursor is: Pages, Word, Google Docs in Chrome, a cell in Numbers, a message about the weather. For a temperature, put the letter right after it: 72°F. There’s no setup, and the same keys work on the British, Australian, and Canadian English layouts.

It works in every app because the degree sign is part of the keyboard layout itself, like the em dash: it’s on the 8 key, one layer past the asterisk. Without Shift, Option + 8 types a bullet (•) instead, which is the most common near miss.

With Mojito, type ::deg and press Return, or type ::degree:: to drop it in without the picker. Shortcodes are the same on every keyboard layout.

6 ways to type the degree symbol on a Mac

  1. The shortcut: ⇧ ⌥ 8. Fastest once it’s in your fingers, and the one worth memorizing.
  2. The Character Viewer: press Fn + E (or Control + Command + Space) to open the emoji picker, click the expand button in the panel’s top-right corner, search for degree, and double-click °. The results also include one-character versions of °C and °F; for everyday writing, skip those (see below).
  3. A text replacement: in System SettingsKeyboardText Replacements…, click + and map something like ;deg to °. It syncs to your iPhone through iCloud, but it only fires in apps built on Apple’s text system, so not in Chrome, Slack, or VS Code.
  4. Google Docs’ symbol menu: the shortcut works fine in Docs, but if you’d rather click, choose InsertSymbolsSpecial characters and enter the Unicode value 00B0.
  5. The Keyboard Viewer: useful on an unfamiliar layout. Turn on Show Input menu in menu bar under System SettingsKeyboardText InputEdit…, then choose Show Keyboard Viewer from the Input menu. Hold Shift and Option and the keys redraw to show what each one types; click ° to insert it.
  6. A shortcode: with Mojito installed, type ::degree in any text field. A picker appears as you type, so ::deg plus Return is enough.

°, º, or ˚? Don’t grab the look-alike

Two other characters on the Option layer pass for a degree sign at reading size. Both are wrong, and both are an easy slip of the finger:

MarkNameMac shortcutUnicodeWhat it’s for
°Degree sign⇧ ⌥ 8U+00B0Temperatures, angles, coordinates
ºMasculine ordinal indicator⌥ 0U+00BASpanish and Portuguese ordinals: 1º, 2º
˚Ring above⌥ KU+02DAA standalone accent, the ring from å

The difference matters more than it looks. To software, º is a small raised letter o, and it gets treated like one: run text through Unicode’s compatibility normalization (NFKC), a standard cleanup step in a lot of search and data tools, and 72º comes out as 72o. ˚ is an accent mark that normalizes to a space plus a combining ring. Neither one matches a search for °, and a spreadsheet formula that strips ° to get at the number will leave them sitting there.

Inherited a document and not sure which one you’re looking at? Press ⌘ F and search for ° typed with ⇧ ⌥ 8. Anything that looks like a degree sign but doesn’t highlight is an impostor.

Should °F and °C be one character or two?

Two: the degree sign followed by a capital F or C. That’s Unicode’s own advice. There are also one-character versions, ℃ (U+2103) and ℉ (U+2109), which look almost the same in most fonts. They’re compatibility characters, and the Unicode Standard says that in normal use, ° plus C is better than U+2103, and that search should treat the two as identical. Not every app gets that last part right, so the plain sequence is the safe bet.

If a spec sheet or label template does insist on the single character, both are in the Character Viewer under degree, and Mojito has them as ::degree_celsius and ::degree_fahrenheit.

Spacing is a style call. The International System of Units puts a space before °C (22 °C) but none before the degree sign of an angle (45°). Most US publications close it up: 72°F. Kelvin takes no degree sign at all. It’s 300 K, not 300 °K, and has been since 1967.

What if my keyboard isn’t US English?

Apple’s other layouts move ° around. These come straight from the keyboard layouts in macOS 27:

  • US, ABC, British, Australian, Canadian English, Dutch: ⇧ ⌥ 8
  • German: ⇧ ^
  • Swiss German: ⇧ §
  • French: ⇧ )
  • Spanish: ⇧ ⌥ º
  • Italian: ⇧ à

On the Spanish layout, º has a key of its own, which makes it an easy slip in a temperature. If your layout isn’t listed, the Keyboard Viewer (way 5 above) shows you where ° lives. Or skip the question: ::degree types the same thing on every layout.

How do I type the degree symbol on iPhone or iPad?

Switch to the 123 keyboard, then press and hold 0. A small ° pops up above the key; slide onto it and let go. Any text replacement you set up on your Mac, like ;deg, shows up on your iPhone too.

More degree questions

Why does Option-8 type a dot instead of a degree sign?

That dot is a bullet, •. The degree sign needs Shift as well: ⇧ ⌥ 8. If you already typed a few bullets into a recipe, search for • and replace them.

How do I write coordinates and angles?

Degrees, then minutes and seconds with the prime (′) and double prime (″): 40°41′21″N. An apostrophe and a straight quote read about the same, but they’re different characters. Primes aren’t on the Mac keyboard: search prime in the Character Viewer, or type ::prime and ::double_prime in Mojito. The rest of the math set is in Math symbols on a Mac.

What’s the HTML code for the degree symbol?

&deg; or &#176; for ° (Unicode U+00B0). In CSS content, use "\00B0". Either is safer than pasting the raw character if you’re not sure about a file’s encoding.

How do I type the degree symbol on Windows?

Hold Alt and type 0176 on the numeric keypad, or press Win + . and look under Symbols.
